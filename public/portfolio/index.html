Described, not hand-wired

A model is a declarative YAML spec or a small Python plugin. Data is validated against an xarray schema, and the fitted preprocessing is replayed exactly at score time.

Every job in a sandbox

Fits and scores run in isolation. Whole analyses run as versioned workflows, from unit-root tests to rolling-origin backtests against naive forecasts.

Checked before anyone uses it

A governance policy requires a model to beat itself fitted on shuffled data and to give no credit to fake noise drivers. A second person signs off.

How I work

The work doesn't start with a model. It starts with a half-hour conversation about what the business is actually trying to decide. Everything after that follows the same six steps.

  1. Start with the decision, not the data.

    Before any modeling, get clear on what's actually being decided: the budget that'll move, the threshold someone has to cross, the trade-off on the table. The model exists to support that.

  2. Pre-specify the model. Then commit.

    Pick a likelihood, priors and identification strategy before you see the fit. Specification shopping is the fastest way to get an answer that flatters the brief and survives nothing.

  3. Validate the data first.

    Schema, coverage, zero-spend stretches, collinearity. Most modeling problems are upstream data problems. Most overconfident answers are too.

  4. Diagnose before reporting.

    R̂ below 1.01, ESS above 400, no divergent transitions. If diagnostics fail, the summary statistic is fiction, and a fiction shipped to a CFO is worse than no answer.

  5. Report what the data supports.

    Intervals, not point estimates. Sign uncertainty visible. "We don't know yet" is a complete sentence. Stakeholders adjust faster to honest ranges than to numbers that quietly move every quarter.

  6. Tie every number to a decision, or cut it.

    Posterior draws turn into expected ROI, the regret of cutting a channel, the probability a budget shift beats the status quo. If a chart doesn't change anyone's behavior, it's decoration.

Three ways to work together

I take a small number of engagements at a time. Each one starts with the same question: what is the business trying to decide, and what would honest measurement of it look like? The conversation is free.

Decision framing

Two weeks. I sit with your marketing, finance and analytics leaders, map the decisions on the table, and write the measurement plan that would credibly support each one. Often the most valuable output is the model we agree not to build.

Build a measurement system

Six to twelve weeks: schema, ingest, Bayesian model, decision layer. You get the repository, the service and an analyst trained to run it. Every output ties back to a decision the business made up front.

Embedded statistician

A quarterly retainer alongside your in-house analytics team: methodology calls, code review, and the difficult conversations with stakeholders about what the posterior says and what it doesn't.
